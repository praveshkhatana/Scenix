# Scenix — full audit and plan (28 Oct 2026)

This is the audit the owner asked for after a long list of failures on the real BRAVIA VU1
(1920×1080, Android 11, armeabi-v7a, 2 GB) against the household hub (192.168.0.24:8788).

It is not a list of opinions. Everything below was measured on the running app, the running hub, or
read out of this repository. The raw evidence is in the appendix.

> **Current state.** This is a dated audit, not the current architecture. The in-process `HubClient`
> and `HttpHub` named below were removed with the rest of the native read path; the page speaks HTTP.
> The native Home/Detail/
> Search/Player stack it refers to has since been removed; the TV app is now first-run pairing plus the
> web shell (`design/plugin-architecture.md` §4). The interface tokens are the ones in
> `design/ui-tokens.md` — 842 px billboard, focus `1.10` / 5 px / 200 ms. The 800 px billboard and
> `1.06` / 140 ms focus quoted below were the values measured during the second pass on 28 October.
> Performance and live-provider gaps are still open where section 5 says so.


## Update — P0 pass landed the same day

The first pass of the plan is in the tree and on the panel. What changed:

- **Playback truth.** `/app/v1/play` answers inside ~5–8 s, returns a 503 `warming` instead of a 200 it
  knows is cold, and the player auto-retries with a "Preparing this copy" state. The native client now
  reads the hub's error body, waits 30 s instead of 12 s, and retries a preparing copy six times.
  The detail page and the active billboard tile pre-warm the first copy (metadata + a 24 MB cushion).
  Measured after the change: Play cost fell from **10.9–16.1 s** to **2.4–8.7 s** on a first press; the
  copies that are still cold show *Preparing* and start without a second keypress once the swarm answers.
- **Home no longer empties.** The lazy-mount queue is per screen. Pressing Home on Home keeps all
  **14 rows / 41 cards**; before, every row was present with **0 cards**.
- **Search.** A two-pane layout on the TV (compact D-pad pad on the left, results on the right) and a
  real `<input type=search>` on a pointer device, so paste, selection and the device keyboard work.
  A live empty state, and the results pane scrolls independently.
- **PIN.** One store: the hub. scrypt with a per-profile salt, constant-time compare, ten attempts per
  five minutes. The native picker verifies against the hub instead of offering to overwrite the code.
  The profile screen refreshes its copy of the household on entry.
- **Adult containment.** Tier 3 now exists **only** in the Late Night row; it is filtered from every
  other shelf and from ordinary search. Verified: a profile with the switch on saw **0** tier-3 titles in
  `justadded`, `top10`, the taste rows and search. Turning the switch on is an adult act in the UI too.
- **Live TV.** `settings.live` gained `hidden`, `groupsHidden` and `order`; the provider filters on them
  before probing/searching; `/app/v1/settings` returns the published channel list and groups. There is a
  channel manager screen (group switches, per-channel on/off, move up/down, paging) reachable from
  Settings, and the playlist URL is editable again. A live channel's left/right zaps to the next/previous
  channel, with a live-specific failure card instead of a source picker.
- **Feel.** Smaller hero (800 px), logo fallback to type, smaller thumbnail queue, a two-line synopsis,
  no trailer grey play glyph (poster until `canplay`), the native video surface hidden on browse screens,
  a per-move focus-layout cache, 18 cards per shelf, TMDB originals rewritten to w500, billboard rotation
  only while the viewer is on the billboard, and a 1.06 / 140 ms focus step. D-pad jank dropped from
  **90.7 % → 50.9 %** (p50 23 ms → 17 ms) on the same 20-step walk; the remaining cost is the WebView
  itself and the next pass is the row recycler.
- **Tooling.** `scripts/deploy-hub.sh` now finds the hub tree, keeps the household's `profiles.json`, and
  passes the gateway environment through. The launcher icon/banner follows the current wordmark.

The rest of this document still describes the state before that pass; it is kept as the evidence and the
plan of record.


### Second pass — navigation, the end of a shelf, and the player

- **Up is a way back.** Entering the billboard or a detail hero now scrolls to the top of that screen and
  lands on Play/Resume, not on whichever thumbnail shares a column. From any row, Up reaches the hero,
  then the nav (Home/Search/Settings/profile). Verified from the bottom of a detail page: Up -> Resume,
  Up -> nav; the previous "scrolled down and stuck" state is gone.
- **Every shelf ends in a See all tile.** The header's Explore all was a mouse target the ring could
  never reach. The last tile in every rail is now focusable and opens the full shelf (`#/search/row:<id>`).
- **Player controls are reachable.** Down now opens a Playback settings sheet (Audio, Subtitles, Quality,
  Speed, Source). The chrome's fade-in no longer makes its own buttons invisible to the focus engine for
  the first 260 ms, which is why the controls felt unreachable. Verified on a warm 1080p copy.
- **The preloader is the film's own.** The player shows the title's logo (or its name), a bar that fills
  as the hub prepares the copy, and one honest line. No spinner, no generic black.
- **Detail page** keeps the prototype's shape: hero, Resume/My List/info, season strip with a real trailer
  thumbnail, episode cards, overview and cast. The trailer tile is artwork with a play mark, not a grey
  square; an automatic trailer miss no longer toasts.

## 1. Verdict

Scenix is not "one bug away" from feeling like Netflix. It has **three structural faults** that produce
most of the list:

1. **Two front ends, one of them slow.** The TV opens the WebView shell by default. That shell is a
   37 kLOC interface designed for a desktop browser, drawn at 1920×1080 inside a transparent WebView
   stacked over a TextureView. On the panel it produced **90.7 % janky frames** during ordinary D-pad
   movement (p50 23 ms, p95 73–150 ms, p99 150–300 ms). The native screens are lighter, but they are a
   second product with a second Settings screen, a second PIN store and a second player.
2. **Playback is a guess, not a preparation.** A Play press spends **10.9–16.1 s** in `/app/v1/play`,
   which is longer than the TV client's own 12 s read timeout and the web client's 14 s timeout. It then
   often returns a copy the hub itself marked `slow`, with `prebuffer.complete: false`, and the player
   shows *"This copy has not started moving yet"*. In a six-title sample, **4/6 returned a 200 that the
   client could not start and 2/6 returned 503**; a second press was usually instant because the first
   press had warmed the swarm. The user experiences "nothing plays".
3. **The product rules live in the wrong place.** Adult content is a per-profile boolean, not a
   household policy; profile creation/removal is an adult act; the PIN lives in two different stores;
   live TV is one 389-channel playlist with no selection or order. The UI is asked to enforce rules the
   backend does not own.

The good news: the item model, the addon host, the ranking pipeline, the gateway and the player bridge
are real and worth keeping. The plan below keeps them and replaces the parts that are fighting them.

## 3. The issue list, mapped to a root cause

### 3.1 Playback — "unable to play any movie/series"

**Causes**

- `/app/v1/play` tests up to `PLAY_MAX_TRIES` copies for 4 s each inside an 11 s budget, then adds a
  pre-buffer wait (up to 6 s), then returns. The 12 s client timeout is not a coincidence; it is the
  first thing to fix.
- When no copy passes the speed bar, the hub deliberately falls back to the least-bad copy and returns
  **200**, with `slow:true` and `prebuffer.complete:false`. The player has no instruction for that
  state, so it loads a stream that is not ready.
- A 503 body (`warming`, `retryAfterMs`, `magnet`) is thrown away by the native `postRaw` on non-2xx,
  so the TV cannot tell "not ready yet" from "broken". The web player does see it but waits for the
  viewer instead of retrying.
- No pre-warm of the actual byte cushion when a detail page opens. `gateway.prewarm` resolves the file
  inside the magnet, but the read-ahead buffer is only opened on Play.
- `NativeBackend` errors carry no `waiting` flag, so a first-byte timeout is shown as "would not play".
- No debrid / direct-cache path at all. The README says "no paid debrid" by design; the product brief
  says "debrid/Netflix-level support". That is a product decision that has to be made explicitly.

**Fix (P0, this pass)**

1. Raise the native read timeout to 30 s and the web play/switch timeout to 30 s.
2. On the hub: cap the first Play press at **8 s** and the per-copy delivery test at **3.5 s**; cap the
   first pre-buffer to **1.5 s**; if the best answer is still cold/zero-byte, return **503
   `{warming:true, retryAfterMs:1500}`** instead of a 200. The reader stays open, so the next press is
   instant.
3. On both clients: auto-retry a `warming`/503 answer up to 3 times (1.5 s apart) with a "Preparing
   this copy" status; auto-retry the same URL once on a network/timeout error; do not burn the whole
   source list on a cold copy.
4. Parse error bodies in `HttpHub` so the native client can see `warming`/`retryAfterMs`.
5. Pre-warm the chosen copy's read-ahead cushion on the detail page (after a short dwell, top source
   only) so the Play press usually finds a full buffer.
6. Add an explicit "debrid/direct" resolver slot to the hub as a first-class source kind, and rank it
   before torrents. This is the only way to promise "Netflix without worrying about sources". Do not
   pretend a swarm is a CDN.

### 3.2 Performance — "laggy, jitter, heavy"

**Causes**

- The default front end is the WebView shell. It draws the whole page at 1920×1080 and keeps a
  transparent WebView over a `PlayerView`/TextureView even on browse screens.
- Every D-pad step calls `focus.move()`, which calls `candidates()` (a full `querySelectorAll`) and then
  `visible()`, which calls `getBoundingClientRect()` **and `getComputedStyle()` for every candidate**,
  several times per press (`groupMembers`, `geometric`, `bands`). That is several forced style/layout
  passes per key.
- 24 rows × up to 30 cards = ~700 card nodes, each with an `<img>`, badges, three quick-action buttons,
  and gradients; `lazyMount` mounts rows as they approach, but never recycles them. The billboard
  rebuilds its text and thumbnail images every 6 s.
- Images are fetched from third-party CDNs at whatever size the addon gave, decoded into 40 MB of RAM,
  with no disk cache, no request de-duplication and only two threads.
- The web player's own `setInterval` position ticker and the native shell's 500 ms ticker both run all
  the time.

**Fix (P0/P1)**

1. Make the **web UI the one UI** and make it TV-first: no transparent compositing on browse screens,
   `SurfaceView` only while a film is live, WebView background opaque elsewhere.
2. Replace the per-key geometry scan with a cached focus graph: each shelf/grid publishes its members
   as the DOM is built; `move()` reads the graph, not computed styles. Recompute on resize/route only.
3. Recycle shelves: cap mounted rows (e.g. 4 above/below), unmount far rows, keep a small image cache.
4. Pre-size every image: request the CDN's `w300`/`w780` variant, set `width`/`height` so the browser
   does not relayout, use one image decoder budget.
5. Kill the 6 s billboard re-render; crossfade the existing `<img>` layers, and only load the next
   artwork when its turn is near.
6. Pause previews and tickers when the app is backgrounded or the player is not visible.
7. Re-measure on the panel after each change; the acceptance bar is **< 5 % janky frames** during a
   30-step D-pad walk, not "looks better".

### 3.3 Settings — "so massy"

**Causes**

- One long two-column page mixing four different scopes: this device, this profile, this hub, the
  developer's diagnostics ("Loaded plugins", "Data client", buffer numbers). It is a config file with a
  theme, not a product.
- No channel management, no category management, no search in Settings.
- Language order is a free-text field of comma-separated codes.
- Native Settings is a second, different page.

**Fix (P1)**

- One Settings screen, left rail of five sections, right pane:
  **Playback · Live TV · Home & categories · Household · System**.
- Move diagnostics to "System → Advanced" behind a deliberate step.
- Replace language codes with named chips.
- Live TV gets a real manager: groups, channel enable/disable, drag-free reorder (move up/down),
  search, favourites. Persist in `settings.live`.
- Per-device settings (hub address, front end, offline pin) are separated from per-household settings
  (languages, rows, channels, addons).

### 3.4 PIN / profiles / adult content

**Causes**

- Two PIN stores: `Prefs` hashes a PIN on the TV; the hub stores a PIN in `profiles.json` (plaintext).
  The shell only knows the hub's; the native picker only knows the TV's. A PIN set in one place is
  invisible to the other. That is the reported "PIN isn't working".
- A profile with `pinSet` but no local hash is treated by the native picker as "set a PIN now", so
  anyone can overwrite it.
- Profile creation/removal is an adult act in the newest hub code, but the UI must ask for the adult
  PIN; the old Settings path still creates a profile with one click.
- `maturity.js` hides tier 3 when `xxxEnabled` is off, but when it is on, tier 3 is merged into
  `justadded`, `top10`, taste rows and search. The README says the only place it appears is Late Night.
  That mismatch is exactly "adult tiles on the home screen while Late Night is off".
- `porn-tube` is an enabled addon with no `adult: true` provenance. The genre table is a heuristic; a
  new label defeats it until someone edits the table.

**Fix (P0/P1)**

1. **One PIN store: the hub.** Hash it (scrypt/argon2 or at minimum SHA-256 with a per-profile salt),
   never return it, rate-limit unlock attempts. The TV keeps only a session flag.
2. **Household admin PIN** (separate from a profile PIN) gates: create/delete profile, switch a profile
   to/from Kids, enable adult content, change hub settings, install addons. A profile PIN unlocks that
   profile; the admin PIN unlocks the household.
3. **Tier 3 only in Late Night.** Filter tier-3 items out of every other row and out of ordinary
   search; keep direct detail/meta accessible only with the switch on.
4. Mark adult addons with provenance (`adult: true`) and default a newly installed adult addon to
   disabled. No content from an adult addon reaches a general shelf.
5. The native screen and the web screen call the same `/profiles/<id>/unlock`; delete the local hash
   path or make it a cache only.

### 3.5 Live TV

- 389 channels, 58 dead, one playlist; the only control is the playlist URL. The row is capped at 60,
  so 329 channels are not reachable except through search-by-genre.
- No numbers/favourites/ordering; numbers are assigned by the health prober, not by the owner.
- No EPG. `live.now` is always null in the IPTV provider.
- Left/right in the player seeks; for live it should zap.

**Fix (P1)**

- `settings.live = { playlist, hidden:[], order:[], groupsHidden:[], favourites:[] }`.
- Channel manager UI (groups + channels + reorder + search).
- `rows/live.js` honours owner order and filters hidden channels/groups; `/app/v1/row/live` returns
  all selected channels, not 200 trimmed to 60.
- Player: live left/right = previous/next channel in the owner's order, with the zap badge; Up shows the
  channel list; no seek bar.
- EPG is a later integration; do not pretend "now" exists until there is a guide source.

### 3.6 Search

- Full-width QWERTY, results below the fold, no paste, no suggestions, and the field is not an input.
- The newest hub commit fixed OK-on-key; it did not fix the layout or paste.
- On a desktop browser the on-screen keyboard should not be the primary input; on a TV it should be a
  compact left rail with the results on the right, like the reference.

**Fix (P0)**

- Two-pane search: left rail (real input on pointer devices, D-pad pad on TV, suggestions/recent),
  right results pane that scrolls independently.
- Real `<input type=search>` on non-TV; `paste` works; `Ctrl/Cmd+V` works; the field is focusable.
- On TV, keep the D-pad pad, but make it compact (4 rows) and never let results steal the ring while
  typing. Enter on a result opens it; Enter on the field runs the search.

### 3.7 Splash / profiles / home "wow"

- The intro is a 1.5 s hold with a logo; it is not a transition into the product.
- The billboard is 842 px tall, so the first shelf is only a title at the bottom of the screen. The
  reference shows 3–4 cards peeking.
- The billboard thumbnails are bottom-right and small; the reference puts the queue bottom-left under
  the copy.
- The first paint of a card is a flat gradient; a missing/loading poster reads as a gray box. Trailers
  can show a gray play glyph before the first frame.
- The font is a proprietary stand-in, not a licensed face.

**Fix (P1/P2)**

- Choreograph splash → profile → home as one continuous move: logo lands in the nav, profile row lifts,
  billboard art crossfades in, text slides up 12 px, first shelf rises into view.
- Lower the billboard to ~720 px, pull the first shelf up, and always show at least a 3-card peek.
- Blur-up/scale-in artwork; no gray rectangle. Trailers fade in only after `canplay`; poster is the
  placeholder.
- Replace the font with an open-licence face with matching metrics (or system Roboto on TV).

## 5. Plan

### P0 — make it work (this week, in order)

1. **Playback truth**: client timeouts, 503 parsing, auto-retry, hub fast-fail + 1.5 s prebuffer,
   detail-page prewarm. Acceptance: 9/10 cold titles start on the first or second automatic attempt,
   with no 12 s dead wait and no "not started moving" dead end.
2. **Home blank**: per-screen lazy queue; pressing Home on Home must keep the shelves.
3. **Search**: two-pane TV layout + real input/paste on pointer devices.
4. **PIN**: one hub store, hashed, rate-limited; household admin PIN gates profile creation/removal and
   the adult switch; native picker calls the hub unlock.
5. **Adult containment**: tier 3 only in Late Night; adult addons disabled by default and marked.
6. **Gray placeholder**: poster-first previews, fade in on `canplay`.
7. **Device icon/banner**: verify the adaptive icon on the launcher after a reinstall.

### P1 — make it feel like the product (next 2 weeks)

8. **Performance**: focus graph, row recycling, image sizing, billboard crossfade, ticker discipline.
   Acceptance: < 5 % janky frames on a 30-step walk; cold Home < 1.5 s; scroll 60 fps.
9. **Settings IA**: five-section Settings; channel manager; category manager; language chips.
10. **Live TV**: owner order, hidden groups/channels, favourites, left/right zap, all channels reachable.
11. **Detail/series sheet**: the reference layout — hero art, resume, season bar, episode strip with
    trailer card, progress on the current episode, cast/overview below.
12. **Splash/profile/home choreography** and billboard sizing.
13. **Standalone parity**: ship a small torrent engine (libtorrent4j) or a debrid resolver inside the
    app; no "standalone" that cannot play the library.

### P2 — make it defensible

14. **Debrid/direct resolver**: Real-Debrid / AllDebrid / Premiumize / TorBox as a source kind, with
    cache-hit ranking, per-user keys, and a "direct first, torrent fallback" policy.
15. **Auth/TLS** for a hub reachable off-LAN; API tokens; no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on a
    public hub.
16. **EPG and live guide** with a real XMLTV source.
17. **Cross-device sync**: My List and row preferences on the hub, not localStorage.
18. **Observability**: `/healthz` already reports a lot; add per-title "why this failed" in the UI and a
    hub log ring buffer.
19. **Tests**: hub contract tests, player failover tests, performance budget in CI.

## 6. What not to do

- Do not add more screens to the native app while the web shell is the default. Two front ends are the
  root of the PIN, Settings and "unable to play" inconsistencies.
- Do not "optimize" by adding CSS `will-change` or more layers. The panel is already compositing a
  transparent WebView; measure before adding.
- Do not return a 200 for a copy the hub has measured as unable to feed itself. A wait is honest; a
  broken 200 is not.
- Do not call the current standalone mode "simple". It cannot play most of the library. Either give it
  an engine/debrid or label it as a browser-only preview.
